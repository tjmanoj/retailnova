# RetailNova demo video script v2 (target 3:40–3:50, allowed 3:00–4:00)

Each row is one narration segment (`s1` … `s9`). The visuals are cut to each segment's audio length.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| s1 | Live Loop: the shopper types the Hinglish prompt; "Sold out at T. Nagar" appears | It's twenty-one days to Diwali. Priya wants a maroon silk saree under five thousand rupees, to pick up at our T. Nagar store. It's sold out. In most retail systems, that moment simply disappears. |
| s2 | Landing page, then the problem slide | Retailers forecast from what they sold, not from what customers wanted. When a shelf is empty, sales read zero, the forecast shrinks, and next season's order shrinks with it. Out-of-stocks cost retailers one point two trillion dollars a year. RetailNova closes that blind spot: every conversation becomes a demand signal. |
| s3 | Shopper chat: a Hindi voice note → transcript → grounded cards with stock per store; photo search | Priya can type, send a photo, or simply leave a voice note in Hindi or Tamil. Gemini hears it directly. Our concierge, built with Google's Agent Development Kit, searches the catalog with Firestore vector search, checks live stock in every store, and can only recommend products the tools returned. The server enforces it. Here it honestly says the saree is sold out at T. Nagar, shows that RS Puram in Coimbatore has fourteen, and offers to reserve it there or notify her. |
| s4 | "Nova remembers" chip opening; a new chat using the remembered preferences; Forget me | Nova also remembers her taste across visits with Agent Platform Memory Bank: maroon, silk, around five thousand rupees. It stores shopping preferences only, and one tap on Forget me erases them. |
| s5 | Live Loop: Notify me → the signal lands in the feed; radar chart | The moment she asks, Gemini Flash-Lite turns the conversation into a structured demand signal: category, colour, price band, and why it went unmet. It lands live on Arjun's Demand Radar, next to two TimesFM forecasts from BigQuery: what sales say, and what customers actually asked for, aligned to the festival calendar. The gap between those two lines is demand his sales data never saw. |
| s6 | Manager: log a walk-in request → Store assist signal; assortment gaps; morning brief | Physical stores count too. Staff log walk-in requests in a sentence or a voice note, and items the store doesn't carry show up as assortment gaps. Every morning, Arjun gets a brief of what changed, the biggest risks before Diwali, and the three actions to take today. |
| s7 | Generate plan → steps stream, including Google Search trends with sources → proposals → Approve → the phone shows "Reserved for you" plus the QR pickup pass | He generates a Diwali action plan. Five steps run in parallel, including live market trends from Google Search with sources. Gemini proposes moving twelve sarees from RS Puram and reordering more, with evidence and revenue protected. He approves. Stock moves, and Priya gets reserved for you, with a pickup pass. |
| s8 | Model Armor blocking an injection prompt; the architecture slide | Every prompt is screened by Model Armor first. Under the hood: Firebase Hosting, an ADK agent service on Cloud Run with keyless identity, Gemini on Agent Platform, Memory Bank, Firestore, and TimesFM inside BigQuery. No model training, no GPUs, and every step is traced and monitored. |
| s9 | Benchmark slide, then the end card with the URL | We measured it. Across thirty test conversations, every recommended product was real and correctly priced, and every injection attempt was refused. In a Diwali backtest where true demand is known, demand sensing cut lost revenue by ten to eleven percent at every capture rate we tested. RetailNova: every conversation is a demand signal. Try it at retailnova-ai dot web dot app. |
