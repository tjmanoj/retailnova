# RetailNova demo video script (target 3:20, allowed 3:00–4:00)

Recording tips:
- Record **one audio file per segment** (s1 … s7), calm and close to the mic, about 140 words per minute.
- Pause for half a second at the start and end of each file.
- The visuals are cut to each segment's audio length.

| # | Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| s1 | 0:00–0:15 | Live Loop: the shopper types the Hinglish prompt; "Sold out at T. Nagar" appears | It's twenty-one days to Diwali. Priya wants a maroon silk saree under five thousand rupees, to pick up at our T. Nagar store. It's sold out. In most retail systems, that moment simply disappears. |
| s2 | 0:15–0:40 | Landing page, then the problem line and the statistics | Retailers forecast from what they *sold*, not from what customers *wanted*. When a shelf is empty, sales read zero, the forecast shrinks, and next season's order shrinks with it. Out-of-stocks cost retailers one point two trillion dollars a year. RetailNova closes that blind spot: every conversation becomes a demand signal. |
| s3 | 0:40–1:30 | Shopper chat: activity chips, product cards with stock per store, Notify me; a Tamil prompt; photo search | Priya chats in Hinglish. Our concierge is a Gemini agent built with Google's Agent Development Kit. It searches the catalog with Firestore vector search and checks live stock in every store, and it can only recommend products the tools actually returned. The server enforces that. It tells her honestly that the saree is sold out at T. Nagar, shows where it is available, and lets her reserve it or get notified. Shoppers can also ask in Tamil, or simply search with a photo. |
| s4 | 1:30–2:25 | Live Loop, right side: the signal appears in the feed; the Demand Radar chart; Generate plan; the steps stream; proposals; Approve; the phone shows "Reserved for you" | The moment she asks, Gemini Flash-Lite turns the conversation into a structured demand signal: category, colour, price band, and why it went unmet. It lands live on Arjun's Demand Radar. Here he sees what sales say next to what customers asked for: two TimesFM forecasts from BigQuery with a festival calendar. The gap is demand his sales data never saw. He generates a Diwali action plan. Four data steps run in parallel, and Gemini proposes moving twelve sarees from RS Puram and reordering more, with evidence, confidence and revenue protected. He approves. Inventory moves, and Priya's phone lights up: reserved for you at T. Nagar. |
| s5 | 2:25–2:50 | The architecture diagram | Under the hood: a React app on Firebase Hosting; a FastAPI and ADK agent service on Cloud Run with a keyless service identity; Gemini on Agent Platform for reasoning, extraction, embeddings and even our catalog images; Firestore for live data, vector search and real-time updates; and TimesFM inside BigQuery for forecasting, with no model training and no GPUs. |
| s6 | 2:50–3:10 | Benchmark slide (eval and backtest tables) | And we measured it. Across thirty test conversations, every recommended product was real and correctly priced, and every prompt-injection attempt was refused. In a Diwali backtest where true demand is known, demand sensing cut lost revenue by ten to eleven percent at every signal capture rate we tested. |
| s7 | 3:10–3:20 | Landing page with the URL; the synthetic-data note | RetailNova. Every conversation is a demand signal. Try it, no login needed, at retailnova-ai dot web dot app. |

The narration is about 440 words, roughly 3:15–3:25 at a natural pace. If the total runs over 3:50, trim s3 first. If it runs under 3:00, add a 5-second pause on the plan steps in s4.
